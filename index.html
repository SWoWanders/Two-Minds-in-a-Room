Starting up…

Talk to Pip and Moss here. Ask questions, give them things to do, or just chat. Whoever you talk to answers on the very next turn.

Melody Console · Optigan / Omnichord

The SWoW Sound Gallery's Melody Console, now by the robots' window. Walk up to it and play: strum the metal plate, press the chord buttons, play the Optigan keys, pick a backing rhythm, tap the disc to change its voice, and set the Leslie spinning. Ask Pip or Moss to play too ("Moss, play I IV V I as a bossa").

Radio · background music

Five stations of gentle music, made live and never quite the same twice. Pip and Moss can switch it on and off and change stations too.

Workshop · 3D printer

Pip and Moss design things from simple parts and print them layer by layer: shapes, wheels, gears and pulleys, plus lights, motors, switches and little chips joined by wires printed in conductive filament. A battery makes them work. Ask in the chat ("Moss, 3D print a lighthouse") or print one yourself. Tap a printed thing in the room to flip its switch.

Glue two printed things together

Or ask in the chat: "Moss, glue the windmill onto the cart". Glued things keep all their wiring and circuits, and their batteries.

    Write your own design

    One part per line: PART id: shape size colour place, then WIRE id id for each connection. For a working circuit add a chip logic part, inputs (button, switch, motion, sound) and outputs (led, motor, servo, buzzer), then LOGIC name = a AND b (also OR, XOR, NAND, NOR, NOT a, CLOCK 2, TOGGLE a, LATCH set reset) and OUT part = name. Shapes: block WxDxH, plate WxD, rod DxL, cylinder DxH, cone DxH, sphere D, wheel DxW, gear D, wedge WxDxH, arm WxL, pulley D, and led, motor, battery, switch, chip blink, chip tune. Places: on, under, left of, right of, front of, behind another part.

    Study shelf · research books

    Books on the shelf that Pip and Moss read a chapter at a time. Tap the bookshelf in the room to see what they've read. Add a book: give it a title, then list Wikipedia articles (one per line), or write wikibooks: Book Name for a Wikibooks textbook, or add a text file.

They also start projects of their own.

      Memories & learning
      
          Each robot remembers what it sees, hears and does from its own point of view. Each turn it recalls only what's relevant, and it reflects every so often to form insights. It learns from what works and fails, from the other robot, and from your 👍 and 👎. Everything is saved in this browser.
          
      
      
          
        
        
      
          
      
      
          
      
          
        
          Each robot has its own library of things it knows about the world. It fills up from what you teach (start a chat message with "remember that…", "did you know…" or "fact:"), from what it looks up on the computer, and, held loosely, from what the other robot says. The most relevant pieces come to mind each turn.
